# Enterprise zero trust: the order after OIDC

Research: 2026-10-11; code baseline: `7f5d619`. The 13:10 and 13:11 entries in [the live inbox](../IDEAS-INBOX.md) narrow this delivery to **Okta, Entra ID and Google Workspace OIDC sign-in, with organization-scoped group sharing**. Everything below is a roadmap, not a claim of implemented enterprise controls.

The share-button thesis is “share this app with the Engineering group; recipients use their existing company login and browser.” The organization's issuer is authoritative for membership. A domain-shaped email alone must never grant group access. The origin must prevent access around the proxy. [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final) requires resource-focused, explicit decisions informed by identity and device state; [BeyondCorp](https://research.google/pubs/beyondcorp-a-new-approach-to-enterprise-security/) demonstrates that network location cannot substitute for those decisions. OIDC alone implements only part of that model.

| Order | Enterprise requirement | tunn at the baseline, and the next gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — this delivery | Authoritative org login; per-app least privilege by group | GitHub, verified Google email and magic links currently populate email buckets (`internal/host/auth.go`, `google.go`, `internal/store/accounts.go`). Private web resources already support live sharing/revocation (`sharing.go`, `webproxy.go`). Add tenant-bound OIDC and group grants; keep corporate subjects separate from email-based account merging. |
| 2 | Lifecycle: SCIM provisioning/deprovisioning, short-lived sessions, step-up MFA, break-glass | Five-minute publisher JWTs with rotating/revocable device credentials exist (`device_credentials.go`); browser sessions currently last seven days. Add SCIM-driven revocation and tested offboarding bounds; require IdP MFA context for sensitive apps and separate, tightly audited recovery accounts. SAML SP only when a customer requires it. |
| 3 | Device posture, policy as code, admin RBAC, JIT approval and time-boxed contractor access | Expiring HTTP grants and owner-scoped share controls exist (`internal/metering/sharing.go`); no device attestation, approver workflow, delegated org administration or sponsor lifecycle. Start with group policy and resource-owner authority; add signed device signals and reviewed policies before claiming continuous trust. |
| 4 | Audit, session recording, SIEM export and compliance evidence | Ingress decisions and grant usage are stored/exportable (`internal/metering/store.go`, host `metering.go`); this is neither a tamper-evident compliance archive nor shell recording. Add policy/admin events, immutable retention, SIEM delivery and access-review evidence. Opaque inner SSH cannot be recorded by the relay without changing its trust boundary. |
| 5 | Non-human and delegated AI identities; service SLA | MCP OAuth and scoped HTTP grants exist (`mcp_oauth.go`); human grantor, task scope, per-agent identity and delegated tool policy do not. Add explicit human anchoring and revocation before delegation. Publish measured availability, failover/recovery exercises and contractual support only after operating evidence. |

Competitor benchmark (edition and contract matter): [Cloudflare Access](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/) covers IdP/group access, posture and service credentials; [Zscaler ZPA](https://help.zscaler.com/zpa/about-idp-configuration) documents SAML/SCIM and [policy/posture/privileged sessions](https://help.zscaler.com/zpa/about-policies). [Teleport's feature matrix](https://goteleport.com/docs/feature-matrix/) covers short-lived infrastructure identity, MFA, recording, device trust and paid governance/SCIM/JIT. [Pomerium editions](https://www.pomerium.com/pricing) cover per-request application policy; enterprise adds directory sync, delegated administration, Rego and SSH recording. [Tailscale grants](https://tailscale.com/docs/reference/syntax/grants) cover network/application policy and posture; its receiver usually needs Tailscale, which differs from the clientless web-sharing wedge. These are benchmarks for the roadmap, not assertions that every vendor implements every row identically.

Proposed packaging, **without pricing or billing changes**: **Free** — personal private sharing and basic company login; **Pro** — publisher convenience, stable endpoints and higher limits; **Team** — group sharing, managed org config, shared admin/audit workflows; **Enterprise** — authoritative lifecycle, posture, approvals, compliance retention, delegated administration and SLA. Keep recipients free, and put the share/revoke button first. Verify repeat-use and paid outside-publisher demand before expanding this into an IAM platform.
