Understanding relay status

Service status shows relay health and readiness observations. No availability SLA is promised. These probes do not test every tunnel, region or sign-in provider.

What is measured

An independent collector requests /healthz and /readyz every minute. Liveness means the relay answered; readiness also checks its listeners and storage. Only HTTP 200 passes. Network errors and timeouts fail.

History with gaps

The collector retains observations for 90 days. The chart shows today and the preceding 89 UTC dates. Gray means unknown. Blue means healthy observations with missing minutes. Green requires all expected minutes to be observed and healthy. Failed readiness is degraded; failed liveness is down. Counts describe samples, not continuous availability. History is never backfilled.

Current state becomes unknown when the latest observation is older than 150 seconds. An unavailable collector also shows unknown. The page refreshes once a minute. A relay outage can make this page unreachable; operators may also serve the collector at a separate status origin.

Incident notes

Operators add public plain-text notes through local database commands. Open incidents remain listed; resolved incidents stay within the history window. An empty incident list does not mean no outages.
